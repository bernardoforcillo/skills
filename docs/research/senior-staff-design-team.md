# How a senior design team operates: roles, process, systems

A senior/staff design team is distinguished less by the quality of its craft than by three axes: scope radius (one team, adjacent teams, a group or the whole organization), problem origin (assigned, self-found, defined at the strategy level) and multiplier output (mentoring, systems, process). The only solid public ladders are those of Intercom and GitLab; the material on Design Director and Head of Design is instead weak and lacks primary sources. The process is well documented by primary sources: the Double Diamond, GOV.UK's discovery/alpha/beta with explicit exit criteria, NN/g and Figma critique, Carbon's "done" checklist, Nygard's ADR and MADR to be adapted to design decisions. On the design system, the normative source is the DTCG format, **stable since version 2025.10 (28 October 2025)** but outside the W3C standards track. For accessibility the reference is WCAG 2.2, in which **2.4.13 Focus Appearance is level AAA, not AA**. Several widely quoted figures in the industry do not survive verification: the "100x" multiplier on defect cost, the "30-40%" coverage of automated tests and the "8 areas of DesignOps" attributed to NN/g. The report distinguishes what is verified from what is inference or folklore and closes with the map towards the plugin's agents, skills, references and templates. The labels [UNVERIFIED], [TRAINING-MEMORY] and "Inference" are those of the research notes and must be kept in the plugin.

## Level is measured in scope and problem origin, not in craft

In Intercom's ladder (30 June 2021) the Senior works in a single cross-functional team, the Staff works mainly in one team but begins to extend influence to the group's adjacent teams, the Principal operates at group level with the other leaders ([Intercom](https://www.intercom.com/blog/product-design-ic-career-path/)). By dimension: on ownership, Seniors deliver solutions, Staff lead cross-team work when needed, Principals autonomously find new problems and improve process and tools. On expertise, Staff build modular systems that reduce design debt, Principals design foundational systems. On leadership, Seniors co-lead the team with the PM and engineering manager, Staff mentor without managing people, Principals co-lead the group (same source).

GitLab gives the most operational text ([GitLab handbook](https://handbook.gitlab.com/job-description-library/product/product-designer/)). The Senior must "bring validated user needs and evidence into planning discussions", "establish clear success criteria" and set quality standards for their own area. The Staff must "define problems worth solving", "quantify the business impact of design decisions", "operate upstream where product direction is set", coach others to become strategic partners, identify systemic gaps across multiple teams and contribute to the Pajamas design system. The summaries from UX Collective and the Rosenverse panel agree in secondary form: the differences between lead, staff and principal are "mostly scope, responsibility and visibility" ([UX Collective](https://newsletter.uxdesign.cc/p/whats-the-difference-between-lead)). One caveat: the older GitLab page gave Staff as grade 8 and the source is a research summary that was not retrieved directly.

For Staff roles, Will Larson's archetypes (Tech Lead, Architect, Solver, Right Hand) are engineering-oriented but useful ([StaffEng](https://staffeng.com/guides/staff-archetypes/)). A domain deserves an Architect only if it is "both complex and durably central to the company's success". Inference (mapping to design, not a design source): Tech Lead as squad design lead, Architect as owner of the design system or core flows, Solver as a "parachute" on ambiguous problems, Right Hand as the director's chief of staff. "Glue" work (Tanya Reilly, [noidea.dog](https://noidea.dog/glue)) is hard to get promoted for and doing it too early "can limit a career". It follows that the agents must explicitly track and attribute coordination work, not take it for granted.

Organization recurs in three models: centralized, embedded and hub-and-spoke, with the hybrid as the most common compromise ([UXPin](https://uxpin.com/studio/blog/design-team-structure)). Designer/developer ratios are anecdotal: Alex Schleifer (Airbnb) suggests about 1:6-8, but a blog table gives Airbnb 1:3 and Julie Zhuo argues that no ideal ratio exists ([UXPin](https://www.uxpin.com/studio/blog/designer-to-developer-ratio); [Intercom](https://www.intercom.com/blog/podcast-julie-zhuo-on-product-design/)). The NN/g maturity model has six stages (Absent, Limited, Emergent, Structured, Integrated, User-driven) ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ux-maturity-model/)). Inference: embedded at Emergent/Structured, hub-and-spoke from Integrated. For decisions the strongest source is Atlassian's DACI: a single Driver, "a single Approver (yes: one!)", Contributors "have a voice but not a vote", the Informed "neither vote nor voice" ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/team-playbook/plays/daci)). That Staff/Principal act as Driver and the director as Approver on quality decisions is inference, not a design source.

Rituals have solid primary sources only for critique. NN/g (Sarah Gibbons, 2016) prescribes roles (Presenter, Critiquer, rotating Facilitator, Recorder), shared materials beforehand, round robin, time-boxing, rephrasing opinions as questions tied to goals, public notes and follow-up actions; it starts at 30 minutes a week ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/design-critiques/)). Figma (Noah Levin, 2019) lists six formats (standard, jam, pair, silent, printed, FYI) and typical problems: crowded rooms, superficial feedback, group "+1", excessive kindness ([Figma](https://www.figma.com/blog/design-critiques-at-figma/)). The Home Office crit is an example with a bimonthly cadence, three facilitators and sessions cut from 45 to 30 minutes ([Home Office](https://hodigital.blog.gov.uk/2020/06/05/running-our-first-remote-design-crit/)). No primary sources were found for office hours, design principles as a practice, quarterly planning and Spotify/Airbnb/IDEO formats.

DesignOps has a verified definition but not the count of "eight areas". NN/g defines it as "the orchestration and optimization of people, process and craft" with a menu of **three** areas (how we work together, how we get the work done, how our work generates impact) ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/start-designops/)). A list of seven areas exists on uxdesign.cc (not NN/g, not retrieved). The attribution "8 areas NN/g" therefore remains unconfirmed. The NN/g survey of 310 coded definitions gives six intents, with Standardize at 27% and Enable and support designers at 22% ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/design-ops-definitions/)).

## From problem to "done": a process with gates, evidence and exit criteria

The Double Diamond (Design Council) alternates divergent and convergent thinking across four phases (Discover, Define, Develop, Deliver), non-linear ([Design Council](https://www.designcouncil.org.uk/our-resources/framework-for-innovation/)). The most concrete rendering is GOV.UK's discovery: "you shouldn't start building the service in discovery", the pre-defined solution is challenged by reframing it as a problem, non-goals are agreed, the current cost is quantified, hard constraints (law) are separated from soft constraints (process), and the measurement of success is planned ([GOV.UK discovery](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/agile-delivery/how-the-discovery-phase-works)). The exit criteria are five (broader context, fit with the problem space, a prioritized list of ideas to test in alpha, alpha team, measure of success) and "stopping at the end of discovery is not a failure" (same source). Alpha tests the riskiest assumptions with throwaway prototypes, typically 6-8 weeks ([GOV.UK alpha](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/agile-delivery/how-the-alpha-phase-works)); beta requires an accessibility audit and fixes before public beta ([GOV.UK beta](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/agile-delivery/how-the-beta-phase-works)). The 14 points of the Service Standard act as a cross-cutting quality checklist ([Service Standard](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/service-standard)). How Might We questions serve to turn findings into questions that do not embed the solution ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-might-we-questions/)); Torres's Opportunity Solution Tree is documented only by secondary sources ([Launchrock](https://www.launchrock.com/library/expert-advice/continuous-discovery)). Jobs-to-be-Done and the d.school "point of view" format are **[UNVERIFIED]**: no primary source retrieved.

The choice of research methods follows three axes (attitudinal vs behavioral, qualitative vs quantitative, context of use) and the product phase ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/which-ux-research-methods/)). On sample size, NN/g indicates 5 users per qualitative study, at least 20 for quantitative, at least 15 per group in card sorting, 39 for stable eyetracking heatmaps ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-many-test-users/)). Nielsen's formula finds N x [1 - (1 - L)^n] with L about 31%: 5 users find about 85% of problems, and three rounds of 5 with a redesign in between are recommended rather than one of 15 ([NN/g](https://nngroup.com/articles/why-you-only-need-to-test-with-5-users/)). The criticisms (wide variance, non-comparable users, the 2001 Spool-Schroeder study) come from secondary sources and the numbers should be rechecked before quoting them ([Syntagm](https://syntagm.co.uk/design/articles/howmany.htm)). So "5 users" is a budget per formative round, never a prevalence statistic. Interviews should use recent critical incidents and not ask for predictions about the future ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/interviewing-users/)); synthesis by affinity diagram involves individual silent writing, grouping before naming and caution about dominant voices ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/affinity-diagram/)). The five-level "evidence ladder" (L1 opinion, L2 single qualitative source, L3 repeated triangulated pattern, L4 behavior observed at scale, L5 replicated in production) is the researcher's **inference**, not a published standard, but is consistent with the NN/g warning on triangulation.

Critique, design review and approval are three distinct artifacts. The NN/g critique "does not simply mean judging a design" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/design-critiques/)); design review is an umbrella term for usability inspection ([NN/g, via research summary](https://nngroup.com/articles/ux-expert-reviews/)); approval is the final sign-off (secondary vendor source, [Atlassian/Loom](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/loom/design-review)). The formal reference model is the GOV.UK assessment: a panel of lead assessor, user researcher, designer and tech lead; a 10-minute demo on problem/users and 20 minutes on the user journey including unhappy paths and offline processes; outcomes green, amber (fix in about three months) and red (stay in the phase) ([GOV.UK assessments](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/service-assessments/how-service-assessments-work)). Adoptable inference: internal outcomes "met / met with conditions with due date / not met" with a findings log and severity blocker/should/could. Citable heuristics with exact names: Nielsen's 10 ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/)) and Shneiderman's 8 golden rules ([UMD](https://www.cs.umd.edu/users/ben/goldenrules.html)); Norman's principles come only from secondary summaries. "I like / I wish / What if" has unverified attribution to d.school ([Third Way](https://www.thirdway.org/thinking-tool/i-like-i-wish-what-if)).

For handoff and Definition of Done the best published example is Carbon's component checklist ([Carbon](https://carbondesignsystem.com/contributing/component-checklist)). A component is "done" at Stable (phases Draft, Preview candidate, Preview, Stable) with: a design spec using tokens only, all states (hover, focus, selected, disabled, read-only, error, warning), responsive and overflow behavior, 4.5:1 contrast for text and 3:1 for non-text interactive elements; code with no magic numbers, configurable strings, working at about 320 px, stories, documentation, typed props; unit tests above 80%, visual regression, automated a11y checks and manual tests with JAWS, VoiceOver, NVDA. Coloplast offers a leaner DoD (WCAG 2.1 AA, axe in Storybook, a11y notes in Figma, design approval) but only from a research summary ([Coloplast](https://designsystem.coloplast.com/latest/getting-started/developers/definition-of-done-fJAqNXuw)). The proposed content for a handoff spec (context with links to brief and decisions, state matrix, responsive, content and i18n, a11y, motion with reduced-motion fallback, analytics, edge cases, design QA in staging) is **inference** synthesized from Carbon and common practice; analytics and much of the i18n have no primary confirmation. The NN/g article on handoff returned HTTP 502 and the "redline versus token" debate has no primary source.

For Design Decision Records there is no UX standard. Nygard's ADR is reused (Title, Status, Context, Decision, Consequences; one or two pages; sequential numbers never reused; superseded ADRs marked as such) ([Nygard](https://cognitect.com/blog/2011/11/15/documenting-architecture-decisions)) along with MADR 4.0.0 of 17 September 2024 (Context and Problem Statement, Decision Drivers, Considered Options, Decision Outcome, Consequences, Confirmation; front matter with status, date, decision-makers, consulted, informed) ([MADR](https://adr.github.io/madr/)). A rationale template for a product skill adds options, criteria, trade-offs and reversibility, but it is a third-party contribution, not a standard ([skills.cat](https://skills.cat/skills/product-on-purpose/pm-skills/skills-develop-design-rationale)). The proposed DDR (ID, status, decider/consulted/informed, evidence with confidence level and method, criteria, options including "do nothing", reversibility and review trigger, confirmation) is **inference**; it is recorded when the choice is hard to undo, cross-team, contested, a deviation from the design system, or based on research that others might call into question.

## Design system: DTCG tokens, governance and health

The DTCG 2025.10 format is the first stable version, announced on 28 October 2025 and described as production-ready and vendor-independent ([W3C DTCG](https://www.w3.org/community/design-tokens/2025/10/28/design-tokens-specification-reaches-first-stable-version/)). It is a "Final Community Group Report" of 28 October 2025 ([Format 2025.10](https://www.designtokens.org/tr/2025.10/format/)), but the FAQ specifies that it is **not on the W3C standards track**; the core is considered stable in production while governance (versioning, deprecation, extensions) is an area of open feedback ([FAQ](https://www.designtokens.org/faq/)). The page `/tr/drafts/format/` (draft of 8 September 2026, still labeled 2025.10) carries "Do not attempt to implement this version": pin the dated URL, not `drafts` ([Drafts](https://www.designtokens.org/tr/drafts/format/)).

The format is JSON with extension `.tokens` or `.tokens.json` and media type `application/design-tokens+json`. `$value` is mandatory, `$type` is inherited from the group, and `$description`, `$extensions` and `$deprecated` (boolean or explanation string) are provided. Groups support `$root` and `$extends` (deep merge, local wins, circular extensions invalid). Aliases use `{group.token}` for whole tokens and JSON Pointer `$ref` for sub-properties. The normative types are color, dimension (`{value, unit}` with px or rem), fontFamily, fontWeight (1-1000), duration (ms or s), cubicBezier, number; the composites are strokeStyle, border, transition, shadow, gradient, typography. Color is an object with `colorSpace` and `components` (srgb, display-p3, oklch and 11 other spaces), with `hex` as an optional fallback ([Color 2025.10](https://www.designtokens.org/tr/2025.10/color/)). The Resolver module for themes and modes is still preview and "do not implement" ([Resolver](https://www.designtokens.org/tr/drafts/resolver/)). Style Dictionary v4 has "first-class" DTCG support but not complete for 2025.10, which is planned for v5; its converter does not rename legacy types ([Style Dictionary](https://styledictionary.com/info/dtcg/)). Open: Figma variables export to DTCG, Tokens Studio details, the `inset` field of shadow and the 2025.10 deprecation policy are unverified. Inference: use the object forms and not strings (`"16px"`, `"#fff"`) and validate files against the dated URL.

Token architecture converges across mature systems. Material 3 uses three tiers (reference, system, component) with names like `md.sys.color.primary` ([m3.material.io](https://m3.material.io/foundations/design-tokens/overview)); the details on the tier structure come from secondary sources and are to be treated as unverified. Atlassian names tokens with Foundation, Property, Modifier and urges choosing "by meaning, not by value"; themes include light, dark, high-contrast and also spacing and motion ([Atlassian](https://atlassian.design/foundations/tokens/design-tokens)). Carbon keeps the same role across four themes by changing the values ([Carbon](https://carbondesignsystem.com/elements/themes/overview/)); Primer separates base and functional folders and themes via `data-color-mode` ([Primer](https://primer.style/product/primitives/)). Inference: three layers (primitives, themed semantics, component) and consumers pointed only at semantic and component, with themes (color) and density (space) as independent axes. The fetches of Polaris, Spectrum and SLDS failed or were not run.

For the component API, Radix offers unstyled primitives that follow WAI-ARIA, composition via `asChild` and controlled or uncontrolled state ([Radix](https://www.radix-ui.com/primitives/docs/overview/introduction)); shadcn/ui states "it is not a component library, it is how you build your own" ([shadcn/ui](https://ui.shadcn.com/docs)). The rule "variant props for small closed sets, composed parts for structure" is inference. Copy-in shifts the versioning and upgrade burden onto the consumer: it is a governance trade-off.

Governance follows Nathan Curtis's three models (solitary, centralized, federated, from secondary summaries: [UIE](https://articles.centercentre.com/?p=860)). Curtis warns that the federated model requires a dedicated central core with enough people, otherwise the system stalls ([Substack](https://nathanacurtis.substack.com/p/the-fallacy-of-federated-design-systems-23b9a9a05542)); Salesforce Lightning declares a mix of centralized and federated ([Salesforce](https://articles.centercentre.com/?p=1980)). GOV.UK accepts contributions via GitHub, evaluated against contribution criteria ([GOV.UK](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/community/)). **No public semver, changelog and deprecation policies were found for Atlassian, Polaris, Carbon and Material**, nor RFC processes (Primer, Carbon, Atlassian): it is the biggest gap on the versioning topic and must be covered with conventions declared as such (inference: `$deprecated` with a migration string, ADRs for system decisions).

Health metrics come from secondary sources and must be reverified: coverage (share of the interface using system components) distinguished from usage ([Design Systems Collective](https://www.designsystemscollective.com/measuring-design-system-adoption-building-a-visual-coverage-analyzer-b5d9ae410d42)), adoption rate with a target of "over 80% at 12 months" proposed by one guide ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/research/team-blog/design-system-success)), detachment as a signal (athenahealth reviews it monthly, [Figma](https://www.figma.com/blog/design-systems-104-making-metrics-matter/)), contribution rate ([Frontend Masters](https://frontendmasters.com/courses/design-systems-management/maintain-adoption-levels)). The targets are blog guidelines, not standards; "time-to-ship" has no source definition and is an unvalidated proposal. Atomic Design (atoms, molecules, organisms, templates, pages) is verified on [Brad Frost](https://atomicdesign.bradfrost.com/chapter-2/); Kholmatova was not researched.

## WCAG 2.2 accessibility and content design: verified thresholds, inferred procedures

WCAG 2.2 is a W3C Recommendation, current version of 12 December 2024 ([WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)); the original date of October 2023 was not confirmed by the page. It adds nine criteria: 2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured (Minimum) AA; 2.4.12 Focus Not Obscured (Enhanced) AAA; **2.4.13 Focus Appearance AAA**; 2.5.7 Dragging Movements AA; 2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum) AA; 3.2.6 Consistent Help A; 3.3.7 Redundant Entry A; 3.3.8 Accessible Authentication (Minimum) AA; 3.3.9 Accessible Authentication (Enhanced) AAA ([W3C new in 2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/new-in-22/)). 4.1.1 Parsing is "obsolete and removed". The minimum target is **24x24 CSS px** with exceptions (spacing with a 24 px circle, equivalent, inline, user agent, essential); 44x44 remains in 2.5.5 AAA ([Understanding 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html); [2.5.5](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-enhanced.html)). 2.4.13 requires an indicator with an area at least equal to a 2 CSS px perimeter and 3:1 contrast between focused and unfocused states ([Understanding 2.4.13](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-appearance.html)): its absence must therefore not be reported as an AA failure, while 2.4.7 (AA) and 1.4.11 remain contestable. Other verified thresholds: 1.4.3 AA with 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text, with no rounding ([Understanding 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html)); 1.4.11 with 3:1 for UI components and graphics ([Understanding](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html)); 1.4.10 reflow at 320 CSS px ([Understanding](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reflow.html)); 1.4.12 text spacing 1.5/2/0.12/0.16 ([Understanding](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/text-spacing.html)). The level numbers of 1.3.1, 2.1.1, 2.4.7, 3.3.1 and 3.3.2 are **[UNVERIFIED]** from memory. WCAG 3.0 is still a Working Draft (update of 3 March 2026) and does not replace 2.2 ([WAI news](https://www.w3.org/WAI/news/2026-03-03/wcag3)); APCA as conformance is not confirmed by a primary source (**[TRAINING-MEMORY]**).

On the limits of automation, W3C states that no tool alone can determine conformance and recommends Easy Checks, then WCAG-EM with people with disabilities ([W3C WAI](https://www.w3.org/WAI/test-evaluate/)). The Deque study (10 March 2021) found that axe covers **on average 57% of issues by volume** (2,000+ audits, 13,000+ pages, about 300,000 issues), against the 20-30% by criteria previously believed; it is a vendor metric by volume ([Deque](https://www.deque.com/blog/automated-testing-study-identifies-57-percent-of-digital-accessibility-issues)). The "30-40%" figure was not found in any primary source: it must be treated as folklore. WebAIM Million 2026 detects WCAG 2 errors on 95.9% of home pages, with 56.1 errors/page and low contrast on 83.9%; it warns that "the absence of detected errors does not indicate that a page is accessible" ([WebAIM](https://webaim.org/projects/million/)). Pages with ARIA have more errors (59.1 vs 42) but the correlation is probably confounded by complexity. The APG clarifies that a role is a promise and that "no ARIA is better than bad ARIA" ([APG](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/practices/read-me-first/)). The nine-step audit procedure (WCAG-EM sample, automated, keyboard, zoom/reflow, screen reader, forced colors and reduced motion, target and drag, form, content) and the Blocker/Major/Minor severity model are **inference/house convention**, not standards; the screen reader/browser pairings are **[UNVERIFIED]** as official.

On the legal side, Directive (EU) 2019/882 (EAA) applies from 28 June 2025 to covered products and services, with an exemption for service microenterprises (fewer than 10 persons and turnover or balance sheet up to 2 million euros) ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32019L0882)). EN 301 549 v3.2.1 is based on WCAG 2.1; v4.1.1 moves to WCAG 2.2 but the sources diverge on the date (2 September 2026 for Deque, adoption 24 August 2026 for DWT) and on the citation in the OJEU not yet having occurred, so prudence advises aiming at WCAG 2.2 AA ([Deque](https://www.deque.com/blog/en-301-549-v4-1-1-is-final-what-changed-what-it-means-and-what-you-should-do/); [DWT](https://www.dwt.com/insights/2026/09/european-accessibility-act-ict-standards-update)). GOV.UK requires WCAG 2.2 AA, automated and manual tests, research with disabled people and an accessibility statement reviewed at least annually ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/helping-people-to-use-your-service/making-your-service-accessible-an-introduction)). ADA, Section 508 and the Italian transposition (AgID, L. 4/2004) were not verified. Microsoft's Inclusive Design principles (recognize exclusion, learn from diversity, solve for one and extend to many) are verified ([Microsoft](https://inclusive.microsoft.design/)); the permanent/temporary/situational spectrum is **[UNVERIFIED]**.

For content design the primary sources are excellent on errors. GOV.UK: say what went wrong and how to fix it, in plain English, never "An error occurred" or "This field is required", same messages on field and summary, without "please", "sorry", "invalid", "oops" ([GOV.UK Design System](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/error-message/)). NN/g: messages near the field, not color alone, no premature validation, input preserved ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/error-message-guidelines/)). Mailchimp separates constant voice from variable tone based on the reader's emotion ([Mailchimp](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/voice-and-tone/)); Podmajersky's voice chart organizes voice by concept ([VTEX](https://uxwriting.vtex.com/docs/principles/voice-chart/)). On forms NN/g indicates a single column, labels above fields and no placeholder as label ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/web-form-design/)); for empty states and loading see [empty states](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/empty-state-interface-design/) and [skeleton screens](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/skeleton-screens/). The checklists on empty state, destructive confirmation and button labels are **[UNVERIFIED]/common practice**; the 30-40% expansion for Italian text is a **[UNVERIFIED]** heuristic with no W3C or IBM source; the GOV.UK figures on reading age and sentence length (25 words) are unverified; the Italian CLDR plural ("one/many/other") is **[UNVERIFIED]**. ICU MessageFormat is documented only by secondary sources ([GitLab](https://translate.gitlab.com/blog/icu-guide)). Raskin recommends undo instead of confirmation, which becomes trivialized through habit ([A List Apart](https://alistapart.com/article/neveruseawarning/)); inference: irreversible actions remain to be confirmed with specific words.

## Visual and interaction craft: citable numbers and folklore to avoid

On typography, Bringhurst indicates 45-75 characters per single column (66 ideal) ([webtypography.net](https://webtypography.net/2.1.2)); Butterick 45-90 ([Practical Typography](https://practicaltypography.com/line-length.html)) and line spacing 120-145% ([Practical Typography](https://practicaltypography.com/line-spacing.html)); WCAG 1.4.8 (AAA) sets the ceiling at 80 characters and line spacing of at least 1.5 ([Understanding 1.4.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/visual-presentation.html)). The Material 3 and Apple scales are citable tables: for M3 display-large 57/64, body-large 16/24, label-small 11/16 (values derived from the material-web v0_192 tokens, x16 conversion by the author) ([material-web](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/material-components/material-web/main/tokens/versions/v0_192/_md-sys-typescale.scss)); for iOS Body 17/22 and minimum text 11 pt ([Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography.json)). Fluid typography with `clamp()` uses limits in rem, with a caveat on 1.4.4 if vw is abused ([Smashing](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2022/01/modern-fluid-typography-css-clamp/)); for font loading `font-display` and `size-adjust` apply ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/font-best-practices)). The folklore: the "golden ratio" scale and other modular ratios are aesthetic conventions (musical ratios beyond the golden ratio are **[TRAINING-MEMORY]**); there is no empirical basis for 45-75 beyond typographic tradition.

On color, Oklab corrects defects of CIELAB and HSV but its author states limits (data generated from CAM16, not from human experiments) ([Ottosson](https://bottosson.github.io/posts/oklab/)); Material's HCT is based on CAM16 and L* ([material-color-utilities](https://github.com/material-foundation/material-color-utilities/blob/main/README.md)). The shortcut "tone difference 40 ≈ 3:1, 50 ≈ 4.5:1" is reported by a research summary of the HCT source and must be verified before quoting it. Apple asks for 4.5:1 in every appearance and 7:1 for custom small text, lighter elevated surfaces and no app-specific appearance toggle ([Apple dark mode](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/design/human-interface-guidelines/dark-mode.json)). Red-green color blindness affects up to 8% of men and 0.5% of women of Northern European descent: it is an upper bound, not global ([MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-9032/13/16/2031)). Folklore to avoid: "never pure black, use #121212", which is not confirmed. APCA is not a conformance criterion in WCAG 2.2.

On targets there are four non-interchangeable numbers: WCAG 2.5.8 AA 24 px, WCAG 2.5.5 AAA 44 px, Apple 44x44 pt default with minimum 28 pt ([Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility.json)), Android 48x48 dp with 8 dp spacing ([Google](https://support.google.com/accessibility/android/answer/7101858)); Fluent indicates 44 on iOS/Web and 48 on Android ([Fluent 2](https://fluent2.microsoft.design/layout)). The inferred cross-platform rule: 44 CSS px for primary touch controls, 24 as the minimum legal threshold. On spacing, Fluent uses a 4 px base and breakpoints 320/480/640/1024/1366/1920, while the Android window size classes (600/840/1200/1600 dp) are **[TRAINING-MEMORY]** to be confirmed; the superiority of the 8-point grid has no empirical proof, it is an alignment convention. Container queries (`container-type: inline-size`) are the reference for component-level responsive ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/CSS_containment/Container_queries)).

For motion, the M3 duration tokens run from 50 to 1000 ms in 16 steps (short1 50 ... extra-long4 1000) with easing as standard (0.2,0,0,1) ([material-web](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/material-components/material-web/main/tokens/versions/v0_192/_md-sys-motion.scss)), a dated token version (M3 Expressive moves to spring motion, **[TRAINING-MEMORY]**). NN/g gives 100-500 ms as a practical guide, not the result of a single study, with exits shorter than entrances ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/animation-duration/)) and response limits 0.1/1/10 s ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/response-times-3-important-limits/)). Reduced motion should be reduced, not forcibly removed (`animation: none !important` can break `animationend`) ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/prefers-reduced-motion)); 2.3.3 is AAA, 2.3.1 (three flashes per second) is A ([Understanding 2.3.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html)). Folklore: "300 ms is the magic duration". On laws, Hick holds for ordered and known choices, it does not justify a "fewer than 7 options" ([Wikipedia, secondary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hick%27s_law)); "Miller 7±2" and the "3-click rule" are folklore, with no source retrieved. The keyboard follows the APG: one tab stop per composite widget, roving tabindex, no positive tabindex ([APG](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/practices/keyboard-interface/)).

## Metrics and impact: correlations, not causes

The HEART framework is by Rodden, Hutchinson and Fu (CHI 2010, Google) with the Goals-Signals-Metrics process ([Google Research](https://research.google/pubs/measuring-the-user-experience-on-a-large-scale-user-centered-metrics-for-web-applications/)); the five dimensions (Happiness, Engagement, Adoption, Retention, Task success) are **[UNVERIFIED]** from memory because the PDF was not readable. The SUS has 10 items and a 0-100 score (odd: answer minus 1; even: 5 minus answer; sum x 2.5), mean about 68 over about 500 studies, and should be communicated as a percentile, not as a percentage (74 ≈ 70th percentile, above 80.3 = grade A) ([MeasuringU](https://measuringu.com/sus/)). SUPR-Q, UMUX-Lite, CES, the limits of NPS and Core Web Vitals are all **[UNVERIFIED]**.

The McKinsey Design Index (2018) reports that top-quartile companies grow 32 points faster in revenue and 56 points in total returns over five years, across 300 listed companies ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2019/11/19/the-controversial-debate-over-the-values-of-design); primary text not retrieved due to timeout). Secondary sources diverge between "percent" and "percentage points". It is a correlational study by a consulting firm with a proprietary index, with no independent replication found; reverse causality and survivorship are plausible inference. Safe wording: "McKinsey reported a correlation in a sample of 300 companies".

The "100x" multiplier on defect-fix cost is not supported. The chart is attributed to the "IBM Systems Sciences Institute", which according to Laurent Bossavit was an internal IBM training program; Pressman 1987 cites it as course notes and the original data are no more recent than 1981, perhaps 1967; a 2016 study of 171 TSP projects found no generally significant differences in resolution times ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2021/07/22/bugs_expense_bs/); [Hillel Wayne](https://buttondown.com/hillelwayne/archive/i-ing-hate-science/)). Moreover it concerns software defects, not design decisions (inference). The 5:1 attributed to Boehm in 2001 is **[UNVERIFIED]**.

On experiments, peeking, sample ratio mismatch and novelty effect are the standard invalidators; the reference source is Kohavi, Tang and Xu (Cambridge UP, 2020), but only secondary summaries are available here ([Kameleoon](https://www.kameleoon.com/enronny-kohavi-getting-results-you-trust)). The skills.cat figures (8% SRM, 26% false positives) are not confirmed and must not be used. RICE (Reach, Impact, Confidence, Effort) is documented by Intercom (score = Reach x Impact x Confidence / Effort, formula deduced from an image) ([Intercom](https://www.intercom.com/blog/rice-simple-prioritization-for-product-managers/)); ICE and Kano are **[UNVERIFIED]**; the limits (subjective estimates, false precision, favoring incremental work) are reasoning, not source criticism. On deceptive patterns, deceptive.design lists 18 types (from Sneaking to Currency confusion) but the list seems different from Brignull's original 12 and must be verified before quoting it ([deceptive.design](https://deceptive.design/types)).

## Conclusion

The non-obvious result is that a "senior design team" is above all a discipline of evidence and decision, more than of aesthetics: the strongest sources do not describe styles but exit criteria (GOV.UK), roles and time-boxes (NN/g, Figma), done checklists (Carbon), normative numeric thresholds (WCAG, DTCG). For this reason a credible plugin must require Claude to declare for every claim the evidence level (verified, [UNVERIFIED], [TRAINING-MEMORY], inference, folklore) and not to turn a convention into a standard. The most concrete risk is confidently importing wrong figures (100x, 30-40%, "8 areas", 2.4.13 as AA) that circulate in the industry.

The second result is the asymmetry of public knowledge: process, a11y and tokens have retrievable primary sources; Director/Head of Design ladders, design system versioning governance, DesignOps, non-critique rituals and design-specific decisions do not. Here the plugin must work by explicit conventions marked as "house convention", not by invented citations, and keep track of the gaps to close them with later research.

### Gaps and main corrections

Mandatory corrections to codify in the plugin: (1) **WCAG 2.4.13 Focus Appearance is AAA, not AA**; do not report its absence as an AA defect, report 2.4.7 and 1.4.11 instead ([Understanding 2.4.13](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-appearance.html)); (2) the "100x" on defect cost is **unsupported** (origin in an internal IBM course, data at most from 1981, and it concerns software, not design) ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2021/07/22/bugs_expense_bs/)); (3) the "30-40% coverage of automated tests" **has no primary source**: cite only Deque 57% by volume (vendor) with the caveat that automation does not judge alt quality, focus order, keyboard traps in custom widgets, screen reader output; (4) the "8 areas of DesignOps" attributed to NN/g are **unconfirmed**: NN/g cites three; (5) **DTCG 2025.10 is stable since 28 October 2025 but not on the W3C standards track**: pin the dated URL, not `drafts`, and consider Resolver as preview; (6) McKinsey Design Index as correlation, not cause; (7) "5 users" as a per-round budget, not a statistic; (8) 24 px (AA) and 44 px (AAA) are not interchangeable; (9) up to 8% (Northern European men) is not global prevalence; (10) EN 301 549 v4.1.1: sources disagree on the date, aim at WCAG 2.2 AA.

Evidence gaps to declare in the plugin: public ladders of Figma, Spotify, Shopify, Atlassian and GDS not retrieved; no primary source for Design Director/Head of Design; semver, changelog, deprecation and RFC policies of the major design systems; Figma export to DTCG and Tokens Studio details; HEART definitions by letter, SUPR-Q, UMUX-Lite, CES, NPS; JTBD and the d.school POV format; Opportunity Solution Tree from a primary source; office hours, design principles and quarterly planning; ADA, Section 508 and the Italian transposition of the EAA (AgID, L. 4/2004); permanent/temporary/situational inclusive design spectrum; GOV.UK figures on reading age and sentence length; official data on string expansion; M3 role-tone map and Material layout documentation not readable (JS-rendered sites); designer/researcher ratios. All the role definitions, severity thresholds, evidence ladder, DDR and handoff spec proposed in this report are **synthesis inferences** and must be presented as modifiable default conventions, not as industry standards.
