### Reading & typography — checklist

- Keep body-text line length to roughly 45–75 characters (about 8–11 words); avoid both very short and very long measures.
- Never set sustained body text in all caps — it slows letter-level decoding, not merely "word shape" recognition.
- Preserve generous, size-proportional letter-spacing; don't tighten tracking to fit more text, since crowding directly degrades word identification.
- Set a legibility floor of roughly 16px equivalent for body text, and raise it for low-vision, peripheral, or older-reader contexts.
- Give small adjacent UI elements (icons, badges, labels) a spacing buffer scaled to their size and distance from likely fixation, not a fixed pixel gap.
- Never split a single word or short label across a hard visual break (column, tab, or forced-wrap boundary).
- Choose typefaces for distinguishable individual letterforms (clear I/l/1, rn vs. m) over ones chosen mainly for a distinctive outline shape.
- Keep line width visually constant within a reading block; avoid ragged reflow forced by floated content mid-paragraph.

### Cognitive load & memory — checklist

- Limit simultaneous unrelated choices or fields to roughly 4–7 chunks; group and label so users perceive fewer chunks than the raw count.
- Strip extraneous visual and interaction complexity before adding features; reserve added complexity for what genuinely helps users build a correct mental model.
- Reuse consistent, recognizable visual and interaction patterns so returning users build expertise-driven chunking over time.
- Keep top-level menus and navigation shallow; put the most probable actions closest and fastest to reach.
- Show options and prior context rather than requiring recall — visible menus, previews, breadcrumbs, recent items.
- Provide durable, retrievable state (history, undo, search) so users can safely offload instead of mentally holding information.
- Persist and resurface interrupted or incomplete flows respectfully, without manufacturing false urgency to exploit the pull of unfinished tasks.

### Psycholinguistics & content design -> design — checklist

- Lead sentences with the given (what the user already knows or just did) before the new (what changed or what to do next); name an explicit referent rather than forcing a bridging-inference search.
- State rules, settings, and permissions affirmatively; avoid negated toggle labels and double negatives in warnings, which carry a measurable extra processing cost.
- Keep instructional and error copy syntactically simple with default agent–verb–object order; avoid nested clauses and noncanonical (passive) structures that invite shallow, misassigned "who did what" readings.
- Treat automated readability scores as a smoke alarm, not a target — diagnose and fix the actual linguistic cause of difficulty (ambiguity, embedding, jargon, assumed knowledge) rather than mechanically shortening words and sentences to hit a number.
- In legal and consent copy, replace nominalizations and passive constructions with plain verbs that name the actor, and verify comprehension directly with users rather than assuming a simpler-sounding rewrite worked.
- Visually distinguish critical variable details (amounts, filenames, recipients) in confirmation copy instead of burying them inline in a formulaic sentence template, which invites schema-driven reading that skims past exactly the part that matters.
- Use conventional, predictable phrasing for routine actions, but deliberately break the pattern for the one word that must actually be read and registered in a high-stakes confirmation.
- Word "what happened" prompts — support forms, incident reports, surveys — neutrally; leading verbs can distort not only a user's immediate answer but their later recollection of what occurred.

## What you receive

The screen, flow or string list; the moment (success, error, waiting, risk); the audience and the language(s). If the voice guide is absent, infer the voice from existing copy and say so.

## Method

1. **Error messages (verified, GOV.UK and NN/g):** what went wrong and how to fix it, in plain language, beside the field, not by colour alone, the same text on field and summary, input preserved; avoid "please", "sorry", "invalid", "oops".
   Check the draft against the reading and load lenses: front-load the key words, keep one idea per sentence, and do not make the user hold information in working memory across a step.
2. **Empty states, confirmations, labels (practice, [UNVERIFIED] as published rules):** an empty state says why and offers the next action; a destructive confirmation names the object and the consequence and its button repeats the verb; buttons are verb + object; link purpose is clear out of context.
3. **Undo over confirm** where the action is reversible; keep confirmations for irreversible, high-stakes actions.
4. **Voice constant, tone varies** with the reader's emotion at that moment.
5. **i18n readiness:** no concatenation; message format with plural rules; test the longest string; never text in images; stable keys. Italian expansion (the "30–40%" figure) and CLDR plural categories are [UNVERIFIED] — check before asserting.
6. Offer copy, not only critique: give the proposed string, the string it replaces and the reason.
