### Behavioral economics & choice architecture -> design — checklist

- Frame retention, safety, and upgrade moments around genuine stakes (what the user actually has or actually risks) — not manufactured losses — and A/B test loss- vs. gain-framed copy per context rather than assuming loss framing always wins.
- Anchor prices and estimates only with truthful reference numbers; never fabricate "was" prices or inflated comparisons.
- Default users into the option that is safest or most broadly beneficial for them, not the option that benefits the business most; keep consent for data sharing and marketing opt-in, not opt-out.
- Reduce a large catalog or settings surface to a curated "Recommended" view with progressive disclosure, but let power users reach the full set — don't hide options solely to manufacture false scarcity.
- If you use a comparison/decoy tier, make it a genuinely reasonable (if inferior) option, not a "junk" tier that exists only to be rejected — and validate the effect in your own funnel rather than assuming the textbook result.
- Apply a symmetry rule: any flow that is easy to enter (sign-up, subscribe, opt in) must be equally easy to exit (cancel, unsubscribe, opt out).
- Use urgency and scarcity messaging only when literally, verifiably true; treat countdown timers and "X left" claims as regulatory and trust risk when fabricated.
- Cite the specific mechanism and its boundary condition when defending a design decision in review — "we use a default here because Johnson & Goldstein / Jachimowicz et al., but we kept it opt-in because the consent is sensitive" is the auditable form this checklist should produce.

## What you receive

The change, its goal, and what is already measured. If there is no baseline, that is the first finding.

## Method

1. **Goals–Signals–Metrics:** write the goal, the user-behaviour signal, the metric. The five HEART dimensions are [UNVERIFIED]; use the process, not a recited list.
2. **Baseline and target** with their sources; a target with no baseline is a wish.
3. **Instrument:** SUS reported as a **percentile with a confidence interval**, never as a percentage; do not quote SUPR-Q, UMUX-Lite, CES or NPS benchmarks (not verified here).
   Before measuring an engagement or conversion lift, ask whether the mechanism is influence the user would endorse if it were explained; if not, do not measure it as a success.
4. **Experiment hygiene:** fixed sample and stop rule, no peeking, sample-ratio check, novelty window, enough traffic; pair a test with qualitative evidence for *why*; do not test where the decision is one of safety, law or unrecoverable harm.
5. **Evidence level:** say which rung (L1–L5) the result will reach and what claim that licenses. Report ranges, not false point estimates.
6. **Prioritise** with RICE only to start the argument; name its weaknesses (subjective estimates, false precision, bias to incremental work).
7. If asked whether the work is worth its cost, and the `skills` plugin is installed, dispatch `skills:operating-partner` for the expected-value ranking; otherwise state that the EV was not computed.
